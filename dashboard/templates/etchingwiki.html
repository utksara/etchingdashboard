Depth vs Number of Etching Cycles

Deep reactive-ion etching (DRIE) proceeds in repetitive etch–passivation cycles. Each cycle typically removes a small vertical depth (the "scallop height"). The total etched depth increases approximately linearly with cycle count until aspect ratio–dependent effects begin to reduce the etch rate.

Typical Bosch DRIE processes remove between 0.3–0.6 µm per cycle depending on chamber pressure, bias voltage, and fluorocarbon polymer buildup. At 8 s etch + 5 s passivation, this corresponds to an average etch rate of roughly 3–4 µm/min. For high-power conditions (bias > 500 V, SF6 flow 130 sccm), rates up to 6 µm/min are achievable for wide trenches.

The scallop “wavelength” (vertical spacing of sidewall undulations) is approximately equal to the etch depth per cycle, typically 0.3–0.6 µm. As the trench deepens, ion flux to the bottom surface drops, so the effective etch depth per cycle gradually decreases (ARDE effect). After several hundred cycles, the rate may drop by 30–50 %.
